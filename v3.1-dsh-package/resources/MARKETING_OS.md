# Digital Marketing OS — Operating Rules

## Core chain
Business goal → revenue/contribution target → audience & offer → funnel → channel roles → budget → creative/content → sales/CRM → measurement → experiment → reallocation.

## Funnel instrumentation
Use project-specific stages; a default model is:
Awareness → Engagement → Intent/Search → Lead/Trial → Qualified Lead → Purchase → Activation → Retention → Repeat → Referral.
Define each event and the source of truth. Do not treat impressions, clicks or raw leads as revenue outcomes.

## Minimum KPI tree
- Outcome: incremental revenue, gross profit/contribution, qualified pipeline, retained customers.
- Drivers: qualified demand, conversion, average order value/contract value, gross margin, repeat rate.
- Diagnostics: CTR, landing-page CVR, lead qualification rate, sales response time, checkout abandonment, churn.
- Guardrails: refund/cancellation, complaint rate, frequency, margin, inventory/capacity, cash runway, brand safety.

## Metric definitions
- CTR = clicks / impressions. Define which click type.
- CVR = conversions / eligible visits or leads; define denominator and conversion event.
- CPL = attributable spend / leads; report lead quality separately.
- CAC = acquisition cost under a declared cost scope / new customers in a declared cohort and window.
- Revenue LTV = expected revenue over a declared horizon; contribution LTV subtracts variable servicing/product costs.
- LTV:CAC = comparable-horizon LTV divided by CAC. Do not compare inconsistent bases.
- ROAS = attributed revenue / ad spend; does not account for margin or all costs.
- ROI = (incremental net benefit − investment) / investment, unless another definition is explicitly agreed.
- Payback = time until cumulative contribution from acquired customers covers acquisition cost.

## Budget architecture
A starting template, not a universal split:
1. learning budget: test audiences, offers, creative and channels;
2. validation budget: repeat promising tests and verify quality;
3. scale budget: invest in repeatable contribution-positive routes;
4. retention budget: CRM, lifecycle, referrals, winback;
5. measurement/contingency: tracking quality, holdouts, volatility.
Allocate based on business constraints and evidence; do not prescribe fixed percentages without context.

## Reallocation rules
Before changing spend, verify:
- data tracking is healthy;
- enough volume/time has accrued for the decision;
- conversion quality and downstream contribution are visible;
- the observed difference is not obviously seasonality, mix shift or attribution error;
- stop-loss and margin guardrails are intact;
- a named owner has approval authority.
Reallocate incrementally where uncertainty is high. Maintain a decision log.

## Experiment standard
Each test specifies hypothesis, audience, control/variant, primary metric, guardrails, minimum detectable effect or practical threshold, duration/sample logic, analysis plan and pre-agreed action. If statistical power cannot be justified, report the test as directional rather than conclusive.

## Attribution and incrementality
Attribution answers which touchpoints receive credit under a chosen model. Incrementality asks what would not have happened without the marketing activity. Do not claim causal lift from platform-attributed conversions alone. Use holdouts, geo tests or other appropriate designs when feasible.

## B2B / high-consideration funnels
Track account/source → inquiry → qualified lead → meeting → opportunity → proposal → win/loss → revenue/margin → expansion/renewal. Include sales cycle and stage conversion. CRM status definitions must be shared by marketing and sales.

## Content / channel planning
Every channel must have a job, target, format, CTA, cadence, owner, cost model, KPI and measurement path. Do not use “omnichannel” as a substitute for prioritization.
