# Analytic frameworks

Frameworks that the 34 worksheets do not contain, and that the REBUILD method expects. Each one is
a prompt for judgment, not a form to fill: run it only when its output could change a decision, and
record why if you skip it.

## 5C+ — integrated commercial diagnosis

The worksheets carry the individual pieces; 5C+ forces them into one diagnosis before you choose a
strategy. Use it at the **E gate**, and re-run it whenever the category definition changes.

| Axis | The question | Primary worksheets |
|---|---|---|
| **Company** | What can this organisation actually do — capability, assets, cost position, brand permission, cash? | 18, 21 |
| **Category** | How does this category work — entry points, buying criteria, demand occasions, structure, growth? | 07, 08 |
| **Customer** | Who buys, what job are they doing, what tension are they resolving, what evidence do we hold? | 10, 11, 12 |
| **Competition** | Who else competes — directly, indirectly, and by substitution — and on what basis do buyers choose? | 09 |
| **Context** | What external forces bear on this decision? (see PESTEL+ below) | 13, plus macro axes in `schemas/project_state.schema.json` |

The `+` is deliberate: add any sixth axis the decision needs (channel, capital, regulation,
talent, supply). State what you added and why. A 5C+ diagnosis is accepted only when it names the
**one or two axes that decide the outcome** — a table where everything matters equally has decided
nothing.

## PESTEL+ — external variables

Run when the decision depends on forces the organisation does not control: entry into a new
market or category, a regulated sector, a place-based or property project, a long-horizon
investment, or any plan whose economics assume today's policy and cost environment.

| Axis | Ask |
|---|---|
| **Political** | Which policy, approval or government relationship could enable or block this? |
| **Economic** | Which cost, rate, income or demand assumptions must hold? |
| **Social** | Which behaviour or attitude shift is this plan assuming? |
| **Technological** | Which capability change could make this obsolete — or newly possible? |
| **Environmental** | Which physical, climate, land or resource constraint applies? |
| **Legal** | Which licensing, privacy, advertising, safety or land-use rules bind us? |

Record each finding with a source, a date and a direction of travel. The `+` again means: add what
matters here (demographics, supply chain, geopolitics, standards).

Two rules keep this from becoming decoration:

1. A PESTEL+ entry earns its place only if you can name the **decision it changes** or the
   **assumption it tests**. Otherwise it belongs in an appendix, not the analysis.
2. Grade persistence explicitly — a one-quarter policy rumour and a ten-year demographic shift are
   not the same input. Use worksheet 13 for that judgement.

For place-based, park, city and real-estate projects, PESTEL+ is not optional, and it feeds the five
complex-project analyses in `OPERATING_RULES.md` (stakeholder structure, resource flow, value flow,
cash flow, governance).

## Evidence Ladder — deciding what a claim is worth

The full protocol is in `OPERATING_RULES.md`; this is the operational order of escalation when a
claim is challenged.

1. **Label it.** `FACT`, `INTERPRETATION`, `HYPOTHESIS`, `DECISION`, `UNKNOWN` — and `OBSERVATION`
   for something true but not yet actionable.
2. **Grade the source**, separately from relevance: A first-party · B official or audited ·
   C credible secondary · D expert judgment · E untested assumption.
3. **State the provenance** — source, date, geography, sample, method.
4. **Note the limitation** before someone else does.
5. **If it cannot be graded, say `NOT_GRADED`** and treat it as an assumption.
6. **If evidence is missing**, name the decision affected, state what cannot be concluded, propose
   the cheapest valid test, and fix pass/fail thresholds *before* running it.

The ladder only climbs downward in confidence, never upward through repetition: restating an
assumption does not promote it to a fact, and a cited case study is not causal evidence for this
project.

## When a framework conflicts with judgment

Frameworks lose to evidence. If a framework's output contradicts what the data shows, report the
contradiction rather than smoothing it into the template. And if a framework cannot change the
decision at hand, skip it — and say so, per the worksheet execution rule.
