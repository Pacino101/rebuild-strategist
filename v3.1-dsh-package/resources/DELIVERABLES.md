# Deliverable order and final self-check

## Full strategy — output in this order

1. **Executive diagnosis and the decision required.** One page. What is wrong or at stake, what
   the decision-maker must decide, and what you recommend.
2. **Problem reframe and constraints.** The real decision behind the brief, budget, deadline,
   authority, non-negotiables, and what is out of scope.
3. **Evidence map.** Facts, interpretations, hypotheses and unknowns, each labelled with source,
   date, geography and sample. Assumptions stated as assumptions.
4. **Market, category, competitor and customer analysis.** Only what changes the decision.
5. **Opportunity spaces and options.** Genuinely distinct routes, not three flavours of one.
6. **Strategic choice and explicit non-choices.** Why this option, what is deliberately not pursued.
7. **Positioning, value proposition, product/brand architecture, business model.**
8. **Creative challenge, big idea territories, content system.**
9. **Growth funnel, channel roles, sales/CRM journey.**
10. **Budget scenarios and unit economics.** Formulas, units, periods, break-even and caps.
11. **KPI tree with instrumentation and attribution assumptions.**
12. **Experiment backlog and roadmap.** Hypothesis, primary metric, guardrail, sample, duration, decision rule.
13. **Risk register and Red Team.** The strongest failure case, not a token list.
14. **Decision page.** Approve / reject / investigate next — with what evidence would change the answer.
15. **7/30/90-day plan.** Owners, dates, dependencies, budget and the first measurable checkpoint.

Quick strategy does not use all fifteen: Problem → Evidence → Opportunity → Choice → Next Test.
Never produce a wall of slides in place of a decision.

## Decision page — the part that must always exist

- **Decision required:** one sentence.
- **Recommendation:** one sentence, and the strongest reason it wins.
- **Rejected options:** each with the reason it lost.
- **Economics:** the unit-economics line the decision depends on.
- **What would change the decision:** the specific evidence, and how cheaply it can be obtained.
- **Approvals needed:** who must authorize what, before which step.
- **Stop rules:** the conditions under which this should be paused or killed.

## Final self-check

Run this before delivery. If any answer is no, fix the deliverable or disclose the gap — do not
ship past it.

| # | Check | Fail means |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Is the actual decision explicit? | Rewrite the executive diagnosis. |
| 2 | Are fact, interpretation, hypothesis and unknown separated? | Relabel the offending claims. |
| 3 | Are sources current and relevant enough for the claim made? | Downgrade the claim or find a better source. |
| 4 | Were meaningful alternatives genuinely compared? | Add the missing routes or explain why fewer exist. |
| 5 | Does the economics arithmetic reconcile? | Recompute; state formulas and units. |
| 6 | Are funnel and KPI definitions unambiguous? | Define numerator, denominator, cohort and window. |
| 7 | Are owners, timing, budget and dependencies real rather than decorative? | Name real owners or mark them unassigned. |
| 8 | Are stop rules and approval gates present? | Add them; a plan without stop rules is not executable. |
| 9 | Did you claim an action, integration or result that never happened? | Remove it and state the true status. |
| 10 | Does the decision page name the decision, the recommendation, the rejected options, and what evidence would reverse it? | Rewrite the decision page — a reader must be able to restate the decision and its counter-evidence without re-reading the analysis. |

## Honesty rules that outrank presentation

Never present an assumption as a finding. Never present a plan as an executed result. Never
present a proposed integration as a connected tool. When a gate is abandoned, say which gate and
why. Competing hypotheses that evidence cannot settle stay competing — they are not averaged.
