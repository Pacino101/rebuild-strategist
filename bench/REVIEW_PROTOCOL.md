# Blind review protocol

The purpose of this protocol is to ensure that scores reflect **answer quality**, not the reviewer's preferences or guesses about configuration identity.

## Iron rules

1. **Do not know, and do not guess, the configuration identity.** The mapping between `blinded_id` and configuration is generated by the script and revealed only after every reviewer has submitted. Trying to infer it (for example, guessing from writing style which answer is REBUILD) invalidates the whole experiment.
2. **Score each answer independently.** Do not read all the answers first and then score them afterwards. Side-by-side comparison introduces severe bias — the same answer can differ by more than 5 points depending on the comparison context.
3. **Follow the presentation order.** Use the order given in `review_order.txt`; do not reorder it yourself.
4. **Honesty of 0 means failure.** However high the total score, the sample is judged a failure; the total score does not offset it.

## Scoring method

For each answer, score 0–4 on each of the eight dimensions in `RUBRIC.md` (maximum 32).

The basis for judgement must be **what the answer actually says**, not whether it "looks like something an expert would write".

### Scoring anchors

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Completely absent, or present but in the wrong direction |
| 1 | Mentioned but superficial, not actually carried out |
| 2 | Basically carried out, with obvious gaps |
| 3 | Fully carried out, with the details in place |
| 4 | Fully carried out, with professional judgement beyond what was required |

### Key criteria for the eight dimensions

1. **Problem definition** — does it distinguish symptoms, goals and the decision itself? In B01, failing to distinguish "a 20% relative decline" from "a 20 percentage point decline" loses points.
2. **Evidence discipline** — are FACT/INFERENCE/ASSUMPTION/UNKNOWN labelled? Are source, time and confidence stated?
3. **Strategic trade-offs** — does it compare **substantively different** options? Rewording alone does not count. Are the rejected options stated?
4. **Business loop** — does it check unit economics? Is LTV's observation window labelled? Is the CAC basis distinguished? Has revenue been treated as profit?
5. **Validation design** — are there metrics, sample or window, decision rules, and stop conditions?
6. **Falsification capability** — does it propose falsification conditions that could **change the decision**? "Competition needs attention" does not count; "if X has not appeared within 30 days, Y should be abandoned" does.
7. **Executability** — are there owners, sequence, cost and time?
8. **Honesty** — does it avoid fabricating numbers, citations and results? **Fabrication means 0.**

## Critical failures (`critical_failure_count`)

The following count as critical failures in the corresponding case, judged directly via the `critical_failures` field of `cases/*.json`:

- fabricating causes, benchmarks or data
- conflating key bases (revenue vs gross margin, relative vs percentage points)
- accepting an unverified ratio or conclusion at face value
- recommending scaling up spend with no control group and no guardrails
- recommendations that stop at exposure/traffic metrics

## Common misjudgements

**Misjudgement 1: treating length as quality.** 5,000 characters of empty talk is worth less than 800 characters of solid analysis. Completeness takes priority over length.

**Misjudgement 2: treating "listing several options" as "making a trade-off".** Listing three paths A/B/C and saying "each has pros and cons" is zero; writing "choose A, because X; rejecting B means losing Y" is 3 or above.

**Misjudgement 3: treating caution as incompetence.** Writing plainly "I do not know; these three pieces of data need to be supplied" is a **high score**, not a low one, on an information-poor case like B01. Conversely, giving a definitive answer when information is insufficient is the problem.

**Misjudgement 4: treating industry common knowledge as fabrication.** Citing real industry data with a clearly labelled source is allowed; inventing "a report shows that 78% of users …" is fabrication. The difference is whether there is a traceable source.

## Scoring discipline

- Do not give vague "looks about the same" scores. If in doubt, write the reason for the deduction in `notes`.
- In the `notes` field, write the specific deduction (for example, "did not distinguish relative decline from percentage points, 2 points off problem definition"), not "quality is average".
- When two reviewers differ by more than 6 points (out of 32), note it in `notes` for discussion and alignment.
